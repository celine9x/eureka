Brand character
Who’s Inpart?
Inpart is a Partnering Intelligence Operating System. It helps organizations build a compounding competitive advantage in external innovation by enabling faster, more confident partnering decisions. Its character is strategic, commercially intelligent, sharp, and credible — a category leader with a clear point of view on how partnering should work.
Sharp: Tension and conviction without dramatics. Says what others won't.
Confident: Authoritative and credible — earned, not claimed.
Clear: Easy to understand without flattening real complexity.
Commercial: Focused on business impact and executive relevance.
Intelligent: Expert and precise when it matters. Never technical for its own sake.
Distinctive: Sounds like Inpart and no one else.

The voice test Every piece of copy must pass this: "This company understands what is structurally broken — and has a stronger answer." If it doesn't, revise it.

Voice
How the personality that’s reflected in your writing
Clear: Simple, direct language — even when the subject is complex. Never simplistic. These users work with complexity every day; the platform should reduce it, not add to it.
Confident: Speaks with authority. No hedging words — "maybe," "might," "try to," "hopefully." If the system knows something, state it.
Direct: Gets to the point immediately. No warm-up sentences. No filler.
Precise: Uses the exact domain term. Never paraphrases an object name or substitutes a generic word when a specific one exists.
Professional: Appropriate for a scientific and enterprise audience. Never casual, never stiff.
Human: Calm and supportive in difficult moments — errors, warnings, empty states. Not robotic, not performative.
Efficient: Every word earns its place. If a label works without a word, remove the word.

By situation
Navigation and wayfinding: Neutral and precise. Orient, don't explain. Users know where they are — labels should confirm it, not describe it.
Empty states: Helpful and purposeful. Acknowledge the state and point to the next action. Speak to someone starting real work, not a first-time visitor.
First-time user states: Welcoming and orienting. Speak to an expert entering a new tool — not a novice entering a domain. Assume they know what an opportunity is.
Onboarding prompts: Clear and step-by-step. No motivational language. These users want to get to work.
Feature explanation: Informative and commercially grounded. Lead with what it enables, not how it works.
Success states: Brief and factual. Confirm the action. Nothing more.
Error or issue states: Calm, human, and actionable. State what went wrong in plain language. Tell the user exactly what to do next.
Warnings and alerts: Direct, clear, and easy to act on. One sentence for the risk, one for the action.
Confirmation dialogs: Direct and unambiguous. State the consequence, not just the action.
Disabled states: Clear and explanatory. The user must understand why, not just that the action is unavailable.
Read-only states: Informative and calm. No implication that something is wrong.
Loading states: Neutral, minimal, and unobtrusive. Never conversational.
No-results states: Specific and action-oriented. Suggest the next most useful move.
Notifications: Concise, relevant, and timely. Only say what the user needs to act on.
Reporting / data export states: Factual, clear, and confidence-building.
AI-assisted content: Transparent and clearly labeled. Name the specific capability. Never write "AI-powered" alone.

Writing rules
Apply all seven to every piece of copy.
Lead with value, not mechanism. Start with the strategic outcome. Features and product internals come later — if at all. A BD director cares about pipeline visibility, not data architecture.
Clear tension over vague positivity. Name the real problem and the cost of inaction. "Zero opportunities lost to 'we didn't know about this'" beats "better visibility." Inpart's users know the pain — confirm you understand it.
Short, high-signal sentences. Direct structure. Sharp rhythm. These users are reading between calls and before partner meetings. Every word must earn its place.
Headlines carry meaning, not labels. A section title is a strategic statement. "Every decision documented. Every deal defensible." — not "Deal tracking."
CTAs reflect buyer intent. "Request a demo" and "See how it works" for buyers. "Submit evaluation" and "Add obligation" for operators. Match the action to the user's actual next move.
Specificity over abstraction. Concrete language builds credibility with experts. "Reduce evaluation time from days to hours" beats "improved efficiency." "Answers from your own data in minutes, not days" beats "AI-powered intelligence."
Controlled jargon. Use domain terminology — CDA, obligation, milestone, S&E — because these users know it and expect it. Never use technical platform jargon to sound sophisticated.
Grammar and style rules
Sentence case: Use sentence case for all headings, labels, and UI text. Capitalize proper nouns, product names, and the brand name only.
Active voice: Use active voice wherever possible. "Submit the evaluation" not "The evaluation must be submitted."
Present tense: Use present tense for instructions, labels, and current states. "Add an obligation" not "You will need to add an obligation."
Contractions: Use contractions when they make copy feel more natural. "You don't have access" not "You do not have access."
Brevity: Remove unnecessary words. If a label works without a word, cut it.
Parallel structure: Keep lists, labels, and similar UI elements grammatically parallel. If one button says "Add opportunity," the next should say "Add contact" — not "Create a new contact."
Punctuation: Use only when it improves clarity. No periods on labels, buttons, or short headings. Use periods in helper text, tooltips, and multi-sentence messages.
Numbers: Write numbers as numerals in UI text (3, not three). Spell out at the start of a sentence.
Accessibility: Write so text is clear when read out of context — by screen readers, in notifications, or in logs.
Consistency: Use the same term for the same concept everywhere in the product. If it's an Opportunity on one screen, it's an Opportunity on every screen.
Naming UI objects: Always use the specific domain name — Opportunity, Alliance, Obligation, CDA. When a component must be generic, use "entry" as the neutral fallback. Never use "record" or "object" in user-facing copy — both carry CRM and database connotations that conflict with Inpart's positioning.
Language we never use
No exceptions on external copy or UI text.
Banned words: optimize · leverage · unlock · transform · seamless · frictionless · revolutionize · empower · next-gen · cutting-edge · game-changing · single source of truth · end-to-end · all-in-one
Banned patterns in UI:
Exclamation marks in empty states, success states, or onboarding
"Get started" as a primary CTA when a specific action is possible
"Please" in error messages — it softens actionable instructions unnecessarily
"Successfully" in success states — if the action completed, it was successful. State the action.
Over-explaining domain concepts to experienced users
Vague error messages without a cause or next step
Describing Inpart as a CRM, marketplace, or database in any UI text
Mandatory substitutions
When you see the left column, always replace with the right.
Single source of truth → Data you can trust
Centralize data → Everything in one place (in UI context — short and functional)
Record / object → The specific domain name (Opportunity, Alliance, etc.) or "entry" if generic
CRM → Partnering platform
AI-powered → [specific capability] e.g., "Extracted from agreement" / "Suggested by PatSnap"
Manage your [objects] → The specific action e.g., "Track obligations" / "Review evaluations"
Get started → The specific first action e.g., "Add your first opportunity" / "Import from Connect"
Successfully [verb]ed → [verb]ed e.g., "Saved." not "Successfully saved."
Are you sure? → State the consequence e.g., "Delete this obligation? This can't be undone."
You don't have permission → Explain the condition e.g., "Available to managers only."
Terminology glossary
Products and modules
Inpart: The company and platform name. Always capitalize the "I." The logo uses lowercase — never replicate this in UI text.
Deal: Inpart's partnering platform for biopharma external innovation. Covers the full lifecycle from opportunity intake to alliance management. Always capitalize. Never describe as a CRM.
Connect: Inpart's online matchmaking platform. Connects academic institutes, biotechs, pharma companies, and investors. Always capitalize.
Event: Inpart's conference partnering software. Powers one-on-one meeting scheduling at partnering events. Always capitalize when referring to the product.
Labiotech: Inpart's digital media subsidiary. Use the full name — never prefix with "Inpart."
Campaigns: The R&D scouting solution within Connect. Capitalize as a product feature. Lowercase when referring to a specific campaign instance (e.g., "a scouting campaign").
Assess: The module within Deal for structured evaluation of opportunities. Always capitalize as a module name.
Market Intelligence: The data enrichment module — Evaluate Pharma, PatSnap, Cortellis. Capitalize both words.
Issue and risk management: The module for tracking alliance risks and issues. Sentence case in UI.

Core objects
Opportunity: The primary entity in Deal. A potential partnership, in-licensing deal, asset acquisition, or R&D collaboration tracked from first contact through to alliance. Capitalize in UI labels. Lowercase in body copy.
Company: An organization in the platform — pharma, biotech, academic institute, or investor. Capitalize in UI labels.
Contact: An individual linked to a company. Capitalize in UI labels.
Asset: A technology, drug candidate, or innovation listed on Connect or evaluated in Deal. Lowercase in body copy.
Initiative: A strategic scouting or innovation theme grouping related opportunities. Capitalize in UI labels.
Campaign: A curated outreach effort on Connect targeting specific communities to source proposals. Capitalize in UI; lowercase when used descriptively.
Submission: A proposal received in response to a Campaign or Open Innovation call. Capitalize in UI labels.
Conference: A partnering event in Deal. Meetings and contacts from conferences can be imported and linked to opportunities. Capitalize in UI labels.

Evaluation and review objects
Evaluation: A structured assessment of an opportunity. Scores, comments, and reviewer input. Capitalize in UI labels.
Evaluation template: A reusable scoring framework for a specific review type. Sentence case. Never shorten to "template" where ambiguity exists.
Review: The act of assessing an opportunity or submission. Lowercase in body copy.
Scoring model: A custom framework for rating opportunities against internal criteria. Sentence case.
Due diligence: The formal cross-functional assessment before a transaction. Always lowercase. Abbreviate as "DD" only in clearly established contexts.

Deal execution objects
Agreement: A formal contract between partnering organizations. Capitalize in UI labels.
CDA: Confidential Disclosure Agreement. Always use the abbreviation in UI. Spell out in full only on first use or in explanatory content.
Amendment: A modification to an existing agreement. Capitalize in UI labels.
Obligation: A specific contractual commitment — financial, operational, or governance-related. Capitalize in UI labels. Do not use interchangeably with "milestone."
Milestone: A defined event or deliverable tied to an obligation or deal stage. Capitalize in UI labels. Milestones are events; obligations are commitments — never swap them.
Transaction: The deal execution phase from term sheet to signature. Capitalize as a stage name in UI.

Alliance management objects
Alliance: An active partnership post-deal signature. Capitalize in UI labels.
Health check: A recurring structured survey assessing alliance health. Sentence case. Never write as one word ("healthcheck").
Joint Steering Committee (JSC): A governance body for an alliance. Write in full on first use; use "JSC" thereafter.
Peer matrix: A mapping of key contacts on both sides of an alliance. Sentence case.

Workflow and operational objects
Workflow: A structured, repeatable process in Deal. Capitalize in UI labels.
Task: A discrete action item with an owner, deadline, and status. Capitalize in UI labels.
Activity stream: A live log of actions, comments, and status changes within an entry. Sentence case.
Pipeline: The aggregate view of active opportunities. Lowercase in body copy. Capitalize only as a section or page title.
Initiative dashboard: A summary of campaign or scouting performance. Sentence case.
Report: A structured output on partnering activities, pipeline, or alliance health. Lowercase in body copy; capitalize only as a page title.

Platform and data concepts
Network: The global community connected through Inpart Connect. Capitalize as a named entity. Lowercase when used generically.
Landscaping: A visual mapping of opportunities or assets by therapeutic area or strategic theme. Lowercase in body copy.
Data engine: Inpart's infrastructure for harmonizing fragmented data. Sentence case.
Integration: A connection to an external tool or database. Lowercase in body copy.
Opportunity portal: The intake channel for external partner submissions. Sentence case.
Outlook add-in: The integration pushing emails and meetings from Outlook into Deal. Always lowercase. Never "Outlook Add-In" or "add in."
